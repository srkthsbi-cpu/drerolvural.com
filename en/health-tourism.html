YOUR TREATMENT JOURNEY

A structured journey from the initial consultation to your return home

For international patients, the process is more than the day of surgery. Medical evaluation, travel planning, hospital care, recovery and follow-up are considered together.

01

Initial online consultation

An initial consultation covers the treatment you are considering, your general health and your expectations. This stage helps clarify your questions and identify the information needed before travelling to Türkiye.

02

Medical information assessment

Information such as height, weight, age, existing conditions, medications and previous surgeries is assessed. The team explains how required reports or test results can be shared securely.

03

Treatment planning

Surgical and non-surgical options are considered individually based on medical history, body measurements, associated conditions and clinical assessment. A definitive surgical decision is not made online.

04

Travel to Türkiye

Your flight and arrival plan are considered together with the clinical schedule. Accommodation, transfers or other services are considered only when they are actually part of the planned scope.

05

Hospital and surgery

After hospital admission, examination, the necessary preoperative assessments and anaesthesia evaluation are completed, the treatment considered clinically appropriate is performed.

06

Discharge

Postoperative clinical monitoring, nutrition, mobility and medication recommendations are reviewed. The discharge decision is based on the recovery process.

07

Return home

Fitness to travel is assessed according to individual recovery. Necessary recommendations and any follow-up documents are shared before returning home.

08

Follow-up

Bariatric surgery may require long-term follow-up. Weight, nutrition, vitamin and mineral supplementation, and associated conditions may be monitored as needed.

TREATMENT OPTIONS

Which treatments may be considered?

Not every method is suitable for every patient. The options below are for general information only; the final treatment choice is made after clinical assessment.

Sleeve Gastrectomy

A surgical method that reduces stomach volume. Suitability is assessed together with body measurements, health status and other clinical factors.

Gastric Bypass

A bariatric procedure involving surgical changes to the stomach and intestinal system. Patient selection and follow-up needs vary by individual.

Metabolic Surgery

Includes consideration of surgical options for appropriate patients with type 2 diabetes and other metabolic conditions.

Revision Surgery

May be considered when clinical problems arise after previous bariatric surgery or when reassessment is needed.

Non-surgical weight management

Non-surgical weight-management options may be considered according to the individual's health status and goals.

AFTER SURGERY

The postoperative period is an important part of treatment. Clinical monitoring and the nutrition plan are prioritised in the first days; mobility, vitamin and mineral supplementation and follow-up visits can then be adjusted individually.

Early recovery

Clinical monitoring and assessment of recovery.

Nutrition

Follow-up visits

Continuing planned clinical follow-up visits and necessary tests.

RETURN HOME AND FOLLOW-UP

Your treatment does not end when you leave Türkiye

Bariatric surgery may require long-term follow-up. After returning home, weight, nutrition, vitamin and mineral supplementation and associated conditions should be monitored. When appropriate, the surgical summary and follow-up recommendations can be shared with your local healthcare team.

ISTANBUL

Istanbul as part of your treatment journey

When your treatment plan allows, Istanbul can be considered not only as a place to stay but also as a city to explore for its historical and cultural heritage. This short guide is intended to help you plan your time in the city.

Hagia Sophia

Hagia Sophia reflects many layers of Istanbul's history and has preserved traces of different periods over the centuries. The present building was constructed in the 6th century during the reign of the Byzantine Emperor Justinian I.

Blue Mosque

The mosque in Sultanahmet Square was built in the early 17th century during the reign of Sultan Ahmed I. It is also known as the Blue Mosque because of its İznik tiles.

Topkapi Palace

Topkapi Palace was one of the Ottoman dynasty's long-standing centres of administration in Istanbul. Its courtyards, collections and views over the Bosphorus make it an important stop for those interested in history.

Galata Tower and Beyoğlu

The Galata area is one of Istanbul's most distinctive districts, with historic streets and an urban fabric extending into Beyoğlu. The tower is an iconic viewpoint overlooking the Golden Horn and the historic peninsula.

Bosphorus and Ortaköy

The Bosphorus separates Europe and Asia while bringing Istanbul's two shores together in one urban life. Ortaköy is a popular area for waterfront walks and views of the Bosphorus.

Grand Bazaar

The Grand Bazaar is one of Istanbul's historic commercial centres. With its many shops and traditional market culture, it offers a distinctive historic bazaar experience.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions from international patients

Can the initial assessment start online?

The initial consultation and review of available documents can begin remotely. An in-person clinical assessment may be required before a definitive surgical decision.

How long should I stay in Türkiye?

There is no single duration for everyone. It may vary according to examinations, surgery, recovery and clinical recommendations.

When can I return home after surgery?

There is no single flight date that applies to everyone. Fitness to travel should be assessed according to clinical recovery.

Which documents should I send?

The basic information requested by the team for an initial assessment can be shared. Detailed medical records should preferably be sent through a secure channel.

Sleeve gastrectomy or gastric bypass?

This decision should not be based on a single piece of information from the internet. Medical history, body measurements, associated conditions and clinical assessment are considered together.

What should I do if I have an urgent problem?

If you have urgent symptoms, seek emergency medical care where you are and contact the surgical team as soon as possible.
